## 1. Trademark Landscape for Blockchain/Crypto

### 1.1 Current State of Crypto Trademark Registration

The trademark landscape for blockchain and cryptocurrency projects is complex, fragmented, and rapidly evolving. Key considerations:

- **Early filing is essential.** Even early-stage projects with limited public adoption can assert enforceable trademark rights. Filing federal applications, documenting first use in commerce, and maintaining consistent brand guidelines are foundational for future enforcement.
- **Crypto-specific trademark filings have surged** in recent years, with thousands of applications filed across Nice Classes 9, 36, and 42 in major jurisdictions.
- **The USPTO, EUIPO, and WIPO** are the three primary trademark systems relevant to Genesis Chain.

### 1.2 Key Jurisdictions

| Jurisdiction | Registry | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | USPTO | Largest crypto market. Must file in Classes 9, 36, 42. Use-in-commerce requirement. |
| **European Union** | EUIPO | Single filing covers all 27 EU member states. MiCA compliance may require established brand. |
| **South Africa** | CIPC | Critical for SA-based entity. File under FAIS-aligned classes. |
| **International** | WIPO (Madrid System) | Enables multi-jurisdiction filing from a single application. Recommended for global reach. |
| **United Kingdom** | UKIPO | Post-Brexit, separate from EUIPO. Important market. |
| **Singapore** | IPOS | Major crypto hub in Asia. Consider filing. |
| **Switzerland** | IPI | Home to many crypto foundations (Ethereum, Polkadot). |

### 1.3 Common Pitfalls in Crypto Naming

- **Generic or descriptive names** (e.g., "CryptoWallet," "Decentralized Exchange," "BlockChain") cannot be registered and offer no protection.
- **Names too similar to existing projects** risk both trademark opposition and market confusion.
- **Using common crypto prefixes/suffixes** (Coin, Token, Chain, Swap, Fi, Dex, DAO, Protocol, Network, Labs) weakens distinctiveness.
- **Failure to search crypto registries** (CoinMarketCap, CoinGecko) in addition to trademark databases.
- **Not securing matching domains** before public announcement.
- **International inconsistency** - a name clear in one jurisdiction may be taken in another.

### 1.4 Recent Trademark Disputes in Crypto (2024-2026)

- **SEC v. Ripple (resolved May 2025):** Settled for $50 million (down from $2B demand). Established that XRP sold on public exchanges is not a security. Major precedent.
- **Uniswap enforcement actions:** SEC issued Wells notice in 2024, then dropped case in 2025 under new leadership.
- **nChain / Craig Wright COPA dispute:** COPA successfully challenged Craig Wright's claim to be Satoshi Nakamoto and challenged nChain patents.
- **BProtocol v. Uniswap (Feb 2026):** Court found blockchain patents covered patent-ineligible subject matter under Section 101, a positive precedent for defendants.
- **NFT trademark disputes:** Multiple cases over brands extending into digital goods (Nike, Hermes "MetaBirkin" case).

**FLAG: The Genesis Chain name itself must avoid collision with the existing "Genesis" brand used by Genesis Trading/Genesis Global Capital (a Digital Currency Group subsidiary that filed for bankruptcy in 2023). While weakened, the brand still has trademark presence.**

## 4. Regulatory Environment

### 4.1 United States - SEC / CFTC

#### Current Regulatory Framework (as of March 2026)

**Significant regulatory shift underway:**

- **SEC Chair Paul Atkins** announced "Project Crypto" (Nov 2025), proposing a new taxonomy:
  1. **Digital commodities / network tokens** (not securities)
  2. **Collectibles** (not securities)
  3. **Tools** (not securities)
  4. **Tokenized securities** (securities)
- Chair Atkins stated: **"Most crypto assets are not securities"** - a major departure from prior SEC stance.
- **Joint SEC-CFTC "Project Crypto"** initiative announced Jan 30, 2026, to harmonize oversight.
- **Token Taxonomy** (Jan 28, 2026): SEC issued statement elaborating that "securities, however represented, remain securities... economic reality trumps labels."

#### Howey Test Application

The Howey Test remains the primary framework for determining if a token is a security:

1. **Investment of money** - Purchasing tokens with fiat or crypto qualifies.
2. **Common enterprise** - Investors' fortunes tied together.
3. **Expectation of profit** - Buyers expect token value to increase.
4. **Efforts of others** - Profits depend primarily on a third party's efforts.

**Key nuance:** Under the current SEC interpretation, investment contracts can **end** when promises are fulfilled, allowing tokens to **transition from securities to non-securities** once the network is sufficiently decentralized.

#### Key Legislation

| Law | Status | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **GENIUS Act** | Signed July 18, 2025 | Comprehensive stablecoin framework. Explicitly excludes payment stablecoins from "security" and "commodity" definitions. |
| **CLARITY Act** | Under consideration | Divides crypto into: digital commodities, investment contract assets, and permitted payment stablecoins. Aims to separate token transactions from Howey analysis. |
| **FIT21 (Financial Innovation and Technology Act)** | Passed House, Senate pending | Market structure bill defining CFTC vs SEC jurisdiction. |

#### Implications for Genesis Chain

**FLAG: MEDIUM RISK**

## 5. Open-Source License Options

### 5.1 License Comparison for Blockchain Protocols

| License | Type | Patent Grant | Copyleft | Used By | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MIT** | Permissive | None | No | Bitcoin Core | Maximum adoption, simplicity | No patent protection |
| **Apache 2.0** | Permissive | Yes (explicit) | No | Solana, Hyperledger Fabric | Patent grant, contributor protection | Slightly more complex |
| **Dual MIT + Apache 2.0** | Permissive | Yes (via Apache) | No | Rust ecosystem standard, most Rust crates | Best of both, Rust-compatible | Two licenses to manage |
| **GPLv3** | Copyleft | Yes (implicit) | Yes (strong) | Ethereum (geth) | Forces openness | Deters commercial adoption |
| **LGPLv3** | Copyleft | Yes (implicit) | Yes (weak) | Ethereum (some components) | Allows linking from proprietary code | Complex compliance |
| **BSL 1.1** | Source-available | Varies | Time-limited | Uniswap v3, MariaDB, HashiCorp | Competitive moat, eventual open-source | Not OSI-approved, deters contributors |
| **MPL 2.0** | Copyleft | Yes | Yes (file-level) | Mozilla | Middle ground | Less common in blockchain |

### 5.2 What Major Blockchains Use

| Project | License | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bitcoin Core** | MIT | Maximum permissiveness; widely forked |
| **Ethereum (go-ethereum/geth)** | LGPLv3 / GPLv3 | Copyleft; requires careful linking |
| **Solana** | Apache 2.0 | Permissive with patent grant |
| **Polkadot / Substrate** | **Mixed:** Apache 2.0 (primitives, frame, pallets) + GPLv3 (client) | Dual approach; runtime is permissive, client is copyleft |
| **Cosmos SDK** | Apache 2.0 | Permissive |
| **Hyperledger Fabric** | Apache 2.0 | Enterprise standard |
| **Cardano** | Apache 2.0 | Permissive |
| **Avalanche** | BSD-3-Clause | Very permissive |
| **Near Protocol** | MIT / Apache 2.0 | Dual licensed |

### 5.3 Patent Grant Implications

- **MIT License:** Contains NO explicit patent grant. Users rely on implied license doctrine, which varies by jurisdiction. **Risk: Patent holders can still assert patents against users.**
- **Apache 2.0:** Contains an **explicit patent grant** from contributors. Section 3 grants a perpetual, irrevocable patent license for contributions. Also includes a **patent retaliation clause** (Section 3, final paragraph) - if you sue for patent infringement, your patent license terminates.
- **GPLv3:** Contains implicit patent grant through its broader copyleft terms and an anti-tivoization clause.

**RECOMMENDATION: Apache 2.0 or dual MIT/Apache 2.0 is strongly preferred for Genesis Chain due to the explicit patent grant, which is critical given the active patent troll landscape.**

### 5.4 CLA (Contributor License Agreement) Best Practices

**Options:**

| Approach | Used By | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Apache ICLA/CCLA** | Apache Foundation projects | Well-understood, patent grant | Friction for contributors |
| **DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin)** | Linux kernel, OpenInfra (as of July 2025) | Low friction, git sign-off | Less legal protection |
| **Custom CLA** | Google, Microsoft | Tailored to project needs | Legal cost, contributor resistance |
| **No CLA** | Many small projects | Zero friction | IP ownership unclear |

**RECOMMENDATION:**
- Use **DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin)** for initial phase to minimize contributor friction.
- Transition to a **lightweight CLA** (based on Apache ICLA) once the project has significant traction and corporate contributors.
- Implement via automated bot on pull requests (e.g., CLA Assistant or similar).

## 6. Legally Defensible Crypto Names

### 6.1 Distinctiveness Spectrum

Trademark strength increases from left to right:

```
GENERIC → DESCRIPTIVE → SUGGESTIVE → ARBITRARY → FANCIFUL
(Weakest)                                        (Strongest)

Cannot be    May be         Can be         Strongest    Strongest
registered   registered     registered     protection   protection
             with proof     inherently
             of secondary
             meaning

Examples:    Examples:      Examples:      Examples:    Examples:
"Blockchain" "ChainLink"   "Ethereum"     "Avalanche"  "Solana"
"Crypto"     "BitPay"      "Cardano"      "Cosmos"     "Aptos"
"Token"      "CoinBase"    "Polkadot"     "Stellar"    "Sui"
"Chain"      "BlockFi"     "Celestia"     "Manta"
```

**RECOMMENDATION: Genesis Chain should aim for a SUGGESTIVE, ARBITRARY, or FANCIFUL name.**

- "Genesis" alone is **suggestive** (suggests origin/beginning but doesn't describe blockchain technology directly). However, it has **extensive prior use** in the crypto space.
- "Genesis Chain" is more **descriptive** because "Chain" directly describes blockchain technology.
- A fanciful or arbitrary name would provide the strongest trademark protection.
